# Human Scope

Write from the demands of this particular subject, reader, and voice. Look beyond word choice to the decisions that organize the piece: what receives attention, what remains implicit, how information arrives, and where the writing ends.

This skill draws on *StoryScope: Investigating idiosyncrasies in AI fiction*, supplied version arXiv:2604.03136v6, 10 August 2026. The study compares published human short stories with generated counterparts. Its findings are population tendencies in that corpus, not universal human traits or a tested recipe for better writing. Applications to nonfiction below are editorial adaptations, not results established by the study.

The instructions here work on their own. For a detailed structural review or questions about the evidence, read [research notes](references/research-notes.md). For examples of applying the guidance without overcorrecting, read [revision examples](references/revision-examples.md). If supporting files are unavailable, use this file without claiming to have read them. No particular model, tool, API, or internet connection is required.

## Establish the writing situation

Infer audience, purpose, genre, length, and tone from the request. Ask only when a missing choice would materially change the result. Use supplied writing samples as the primary voice reference; preserve their humor, directness, regional usage, and unusual wording when these work.

For revision, identify the facts, intent, point of view, chronology, quotations, and required wording that must survive. A light edit does not authorize a new plot. Make structural changes within the requested scope; if a deeper repair would exceed it, briefly identify that need separately when useful.

Choose the relevant mode:

- **Fiction:** Apply the narrative guidance below. Invent within the user's premise and constraints.
- **Nonfiction, including personal essays:** Preserve evidence, actual chronology, and the distinction between observation and interpretation. Never invent experiences, quotations, opinions for the author, or supporting details.
- **Notes and explanations:** Follow the learner's questions and dependencies between concepts. Define terms, explain mechanisms, and retain necessary summaries and ordered steps.
- **Emails and practical messages:** Make the request, decision, or useful information easy to find. Do not add literary techniques to a message that needs two sentences.

Explicit user requirements and the destination's conventions take precedence over this skill's defaults. Apply only the guidance relevant to the task.

## Repair construction before polishing sentences

For substantial drafting, identify the piece's underlying content before choosing elegant wording. In nonfiction this may be a claim, its evidence, and its consequence. In fiction it may be a character's immediate purpose, competing pressures, what changes, and what the reader knows at each point. Keep this preparation brief and do not output it unless requested.

For substantial revision, ask what remains if the prose is reduced to plain statements. If it is still a generic lesson, a sequence of convenient events, or an argument without evidence, replacing adjectives will not repair it. Revise the underlying selection and arrangement of material.

Change a pattern because it harms this piece, not because it appears on a list. Human authors also write linear plots, sensory passages, triads, morals, and happy endings. Keep any of these when they serve the work. Do not optimize for detection scores, claim human authorship, or promise that generated writing is indistinguishable from human writing.

## Fiction: broaden the choices available

### Let meaning emerge without explaining it repeatedly

Check whether the narrator, dialogue, imagery, and ending all announce the same interpretation. Remove redundant explanations and allow actions or consequences to carry meaning. A reader may understand what happened without being told what to think about it.

Characters should speak to pursue something in the scene: an answer, an excuse, permission, reassurance, a practical arrangement. Do not turn every exchange into a polished debate about the story's theme. Keep philosophical discussion when it fits the characters and genre.

Permit details and relationships that do not prove the central thesis. Do not replace thematic coherence with arbitrary digressions. Fables, allegories, and deliberately didactic stories may state their morals directly.

### Give people motives and limits beyond their role in the plot

Let supporting characters have concerns that matter to them. Where appropriate, let a protagonist's choice help one person while costing another; do not automatically explain away selfishness or settle the narrator's judgment. Moral ambiguity is an option, not a requirement to make everyone cruel or indecisive.

Introduce a character through whichever encounter matters: speech, an action, someone else's account, a thought, or appearance. Avoid automatically starting every introduction with a physical inventory. Do not make every opening begin in the middle of danger as the replacement formula.

### Use an appropriate range of emotional expression

Use direct naming, action, dialogue, bodily sensation, reflection, or silence according to perspective and emphasis. "She was frightened" can be exactly right. Repeated tightening throats, held breaths, clenching chests, and trembling hands can become interchangeable even when each is plausible.

If a bodily detail reveals something specific, retain it. If it merely translates an already clear emotion into a familiar physical cue, simplify or remove it. Avoid replacing every body cue with another visible gesture; that only changes the recurring formula.

### Choose detail through attention

Describe what this viewpoint would notice in this situation. Smell, weather, and texture belong when they orient the reader, affect action, distinguish a place, or matter to the character. Do not tour every sense on arrival in every scene.

The environment need not echo the character's mood. Nature can remain ordinary during grief; a bleak place can host a pleasant conversation. Neither correspondence nor mismatch should become compulsory.

Give enough spatial grounding to follow events. Save finer geography for when it matters. A richly observed landscape or deeply interior narration remains appropriate when it is the point of the work.

### Distinguish event order from disclosure order

Choose where the reader enters and when earlier events become relevant. A recollection or time jump should change the present scene's meaning, pressure, or interpretation. A revelation should, where appropriate, alter the reader's understanding of something already encountered.

Preserve a comprehensible underlying timeline. Do not scramble events to manufacture unpredictability or hide information that a viewpoint would plainly disclose without a narrative reason. A clear chronological story may be the best choice.

### Let consequences exceed one person's realization

Check whether every obstacle disappears because the protagonist understands a lesson or makes a brave choice. Other people, institutions, timing, chance, and material limits can affect outcomes. Establish those forces sufficiently that the result makes sense. Do not replace convenient personal triumph with a convenient accident.

Use secondary threads when they create a meaningful relationship, competing demand, or consequence. Do not add subplots, characters, or locations merely to increase variety. Change the intensity and kind of events as the story requires; quiet stories need not acquire a dramatic climax.

### End at the appropriate point of consequence

An insight need not repair a relationship, pay a debt, or erase harm. Check whether the resolution changes the actual situation or simply supplies a reassuring interpretation. Keep the ending the premise earns, including a decisive or happy ending when appropriate.

Remove an epilogue or final moral if it only repeats the scene's meaning or accounts for every future. Leave a question open when that uncertainty belongs to the story. Do not stop prematurely just to sound literary.

### Allow a particular cultural world and narrator

Use named works, places, objects, and institutions when they belong to the character's knowledge and the setting. Do not retreat automatically into vague literary allusions. Verify real-world claims when needed; do not invent quotations or imply nonexistent sources. Clearly fictional references are acceptable inside fiction.

A narrator may address the reader when that relationship is part of the voice. Do not insert "dear reader," brands, literary names, or fourth-wall breaks as evidence of humanness. Respect requested anonymity and generalization.

## Nonfiction: transfer the useful principle, preserve clarity

These are adaptations of the research, supported by ordinary editorial judgment:

- Replace claims of significance with the relevant mechanism, observation, or consequence. Keep interpretations proportionate to the evidence.
- Separate what happened, what someone reports, what the author infers, and what is unknown. Do not give a complicated event a single convenient cause or an unsupported lesson.
- Give each paragraph a purpose and space proportional to its content. Some explanations need repeated terminology or a recap. Remove repetition only when it adds nothing.
- Use concrete examples whose facts are supplied or verified. Label hypothetical examples. Do not invent dates, measurements, sources, personal memories, or dialogue to make a piece feel lived-in.
- Name sources and objects precisely when appropriate. Preserve privacy and anonymization requirements. Generic labels are correct when the reader needs a general explanation.
- Close with the decision, unresolved question, action, or synthesis the task needs. A report may require recommendations; a tutorial may require a recap. Omit automatic inspiration and unrelated speculation.

Do not transfer fictional ambiguity, delayed disclosure, unreliable narration, or disrupted chronology into instructions that depend on clarity. For teaching, explicitly explaining a concept is useful. Remove redundant moralizing, not necessary instruction.

## Finish the prose without manufacturing irregularity

Use vocabulary, contractions, punctuation, and paragraphing suited to the voice. Prefer precise nouns and plain verbs where they do the job. Keep technical terms when they carry necessary meaning.

Inspect clusters of stock praise, abstract significance, generic openings, repetitive contrasts, decorative transitions, and conclusions that restate the whole piece. Repair the underlying empty statement rather than exchanging it for a more unusual synonym.

Let sentence length follow the thought. Do not alternate lengths by formula, force fragments, ban groups of three, substitute unusual collocations for their own sake, or insert errors. Punctuation and common words are not authorship tests. Honor a requested punctuation style without presenting it as a scientific finding.

Use headings, lists, and emphasis when they help readers navigate. Preserve a required template. Do not impose equal-length sections or strip useful structure merely to make the page look less regular.

## Final review and delivery

Before returning substantial work, check:

- **Meaning:** Have facts, intent, voice, required wording, and uncertainty survived? Is each addition warranted by the task?
- **Construction:** Is an automatic pattern actually weakening the piece? Has the revision fixed it without creating an opposite formula?
- **Reader:** Is the information available when needed? Are any omissions productive, or merely confusing?
- **Language:** Are details specific, emotions appropriately expressed, and repetitions purposeful? Does the structure fit the content?
- **Ending:** Does the piece fulfill its promise and stop at a useful point?

Make the smallest revision that solves the real problem. Return the requested writing. Include an editorial explanation only when asked or when a material unresolved issue needs the user's attention. Do not append detector scores, self-evaluation, compliance claims, or routine offers to continue.
